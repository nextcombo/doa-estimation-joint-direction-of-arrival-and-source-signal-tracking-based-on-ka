# [DOA Estimation] Joint Direction of Arrival and Source Signal Tracking Based on Kalman Filter, Regularized QRD RLS Algorithms [with MATLAB Code and Report]

Introduction: The technology of using an array antenna to estimate and track multiple targets in terms of direction of arrival (DOA) is of significant importance in various commercial and military applications such as communication, sonar, air traffic control, and electronic reconnaissance. Many algorithms, such as beamforming-based techniques [2], subspace-based techniques [3], and sparseness-based techniques [4], have been proposed to solve the DOA estimation problem. Most of these techniques rely on subspace or feature-based information that can be derived from the eigenvalue decomposition of the sampled output covariance matrix or from the singular value decomposition of the array data matrix. This also prompts researchers to develop subspace-based tracking techniques for moving targets (see, e.g., [5, Section XII]). Instead of updating when new data arrives, the entire feature space uses the new data, while subspace tracking techniques only process the signal or noise subspace to reduce computational complexity and storage requirements. A class of effective methods is the projection approximation subspace tracking (PAST) method and its variants [6]. The PAST method finds the signal subspace by minimizing the weighted least squares (LS) error between the received data and the projected approximation constructed from the current subspace estimate recursively. To track rapidly changing subspaces, a subspace tracking method based on the Kalman filter (KF) for estimating the subspace using prior information about the state dynamics has recently been proposed [7]. Although subspace-based methods are attractive due to their good performance and low complexity, they are at least limited by two factors. First, since subspace-based techniques require the signal subspace dimension to be less than the overall space dimension, it is impossible to handle scenarios where the number of sources equals or exceeds the number of antennas. Second, subspace-based techniques are developed based on the principle that more snapshots and sensors lead to better performance. Unfortunately, for moving targets, increasing the number of array sensors becomes even more important because such targets can cause the array spatial spectrum to expand [8].
The purpose of this paper is to develop an effective DOA estimation and tracking scheme to address some of the limitations mentioned above. The fundamental idea of this method is to fully utilize the dynamic characteristics of the source signal, and to employ Kalman filtering to track the statistical characteristics of the source signal. Specifically, we assume that the source is represented by an autoregressive (AR) model. For example, speech and communication signals are often recognized as AR processes [9]. In fact, DOA estimation and tracking play a very important role in microphone array processing for speech signals [10]. It also finds many applications in mobile communication systems [11]. These practical applications strongly motivate our research on DOA estimation and tracking of sources modeled by AR processes. This method employs a recursive least squares (QRD-RLS) algorithm based on QR decomposition and a recursive Kalman filter (KF) to estimate the source direction vector and AR coefficients. Within each time interval, we first use the dynamic model of the time-varying AR source and the Kalman filter to predict the temporal state information of each target along the estimated AR coefficients and DOA from the previous time interval. Then, using the relationship between the signal source and the AR coefficients or DOA, we update the AR coefficients and DOA using the QRD-based RLS algorithm, which can iteratively improve accuracy.
Article illustrations
Conclusion This paper proposes a DOA estimation and tracking method based on Kalman filtering and QRD-RLS algorithm. Due to the state information based on AR model, we can utilize an elegant KF to obtain time information, then use an efficient QRD-RLS to estimate the direction of arrival (DOA) and AR coefficients. In the real domain, the DOA estimation is realized by using an unitary transformation, and the DOA is estimated by calculating M sets of real vectors. Simulation results show that this method has good performance in DOA estimation and tracking, which is superior to the subspace-based methods.
